Update formula and final iteration

r^(t+1) = α (Qᵀ p^t + d_t v) + (1 − α) v
d_t = sum of p^t over zero-degree nodes; v = one-hot at user 1
I100 = sum over 377 users u linked to Fargo of p_u^(20) / degree(u)
v100 = 0; α = 0.50

Q divides each source node’s mass equally across its positive training links. The implementation uses rounded Float32 inverse degrees, multiplication and matrix reduction. Numerical differences and reproducibility

The Float32 WebGL rerun matches the saved Fargo value exactly (difference 0), and the instrumented and uninstrumented batch runs have maximum absolute difference 0 across all entries. The normalization correction is included in the worked arithmetic, not omitted.

Summing the 377 captured Float32 per-link terms in Float64 gives 0.001371042128823774; the actual GPU incoming value is 0.0013710429193452, a difference of 7.905214260972571e-10. This is the observed matrix-reduction rounding difference. The independent full Float64 solver stops after 21 iterations and gives Fargo score 0.000685520996303016, differing from the saved Float32 result by -4.0516192310879884e-10. It is a precision cross-check, not the reported production score.

The Float64 sum of the captured final Float32 node values is 0.999999951365993. Thus the mass correction preserves the intended partition targets to numerical tolerance; it does not make a Float64 sum of rounded outputs exactly one.
