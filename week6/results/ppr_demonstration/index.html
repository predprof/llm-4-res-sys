Observed changes

  • Setting 1 vs 2: fixed alpha .85 and graph, user 1 to user 2; 0/10 shared movies; mean popularity 245.5 to 254.9. Per-user test filtering also changes; all ten user-2 movies are excluded for user 1.
  • Setting 1 vs 3: fixed user 1, graph and exclusions, alpha .85 to .50; 7/10 shared movies; mean popularity 245.5 to 228.4. Scream, Air Force One and Titanic leave; Trainspotting, Sense and Sensibility and E.T. enter.
  • The first six titles keep the same order when alpha changes. Star Trek: First Contact moves from rank 9 to rank 7.
  • For this user and graph, higher alpha gives the more popular of these two lists. This does not show that either list is better, or that alpha has a universal relationship with popularity.
  • Scores are long-run walk probabilities. Differences in score magnitudes across alpha settings are not changes in predicted rating or recommendation quality.
  • These are descriptive demonstrations. The primary final evaluation continues to use validation-selected alpha .50; the demonstrations do not select or replace it.

Candidate pool: 1,611 training movies. Exclude every training- or validation-rated movie regardless of rating; rank by descending score, with movie ID ascending for exact ties. All three lists contain ten movies with positive scores. Training popularity counts distinct users whose training rating is ≥ 4.0. Unknown test ratings remain unobserved.

Alpha changes the probability of following a link; 1 − alpha is restart. The TensorFlow.js scores below matched independent float64 rankings on the same graph. The CSV keeps full exported precision; tables show 10 significant digits.
